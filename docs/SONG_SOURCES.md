# Built-in song sources

Every built-in song uses a public-domain melody and public-domain words. No recordings or modern arrangements are used: the accompaniment is generated in the browser from chord symbols written for this project, and each melody was re-keyed into the project's own notation from the scores below (third-party score files are not shipped).

US rule applied: works published in 1930 or earlier are in the public domain (see [PD Info](https://www.pdinfo.com/)).

## Choices and caveats

- **When the Saints Go Marching In** was removed in v2. It is a traditional spiritual first recorded in 1923 and widely treated as public domain, but there is no known pre-1931 printing of the exact melody and words ([PD Info](https://www.pdinfo.com/search-pd-songs.php?S=When+The+Saints)), so it does not meet the bar of a verified, dated public-domain publication. Copyrighted arrangements of it also exist (for example Stamps-Baxter, 1937).
- **Ode to Joy** uses the unsyncopated hymn form in bar 12 (D4 E4 A3, half note), which keeps the phrase boundary clean for practice. Beethoven's original anticipates "Giv-" on beat 4 of bar 12. The alignment of van Dyke's verse 1 to the notes was done for this project (melismas on "and" and "the" in line 3).
- **Amazing Grace** follows Excell's hymnal form, which differs from the folk-session form in line 3 ("I once was lost": D5 B4 D5 B4 G4 D4 E4 G4 G4 E4 D4).
- **Auld Lang Syne** uses "my dear" in the chorus (Burns texts give both "my dear" and "my jo").
- **Are You Sleeping?** uses the traditional English words with "Ding, dang, dong" (versions vary).
